02 / RANDOM GEOMETRY

A local Poisson event. Not an infinite-network theorem.

Conditioned illustration: one point in each small disc, no other point inside B(0,3). Disc halos are enlarged for visibility. Outside points are an independent Poisson sample in the displayed annulus; their edges are not shown.

Exactly positive, potentially extremely rare

pK = (λπrK²)3K+1 exp(−9λπ)

The radius must preserve the geometric inequalities, separate the discs and keep every face circumdisc inside the guard window. The analytic proof chooses it sufficiently small. This visualisation uses a small radius within the displayed perturbation budget; floating-point rendering is not a certificate.

For M disjoint windows the probability of at least one success is 1−(1−pK)M. Infinitely many successes almost surely do not make a typical window a success.

The autonomous queueing model uses only the induced finite graph. External traffic and feedback through the rest of the Poisson–Delaunay network are outside this result.

03 / RESEARCH PROGRAMME

Sparsity is not the absence of hubs.

Current mechanism

First-step profiles, a recurrent background, a terminal bottleneck and a controlled stationary correlation correction.

Geometric realisations

Open families of Euclidean Delaunay wheels for every K ≥ 9. Linear edge growth, but the central degree is 3K.

Separate questions

Bounded-degree or hub-free networks, non-terminal feedback, moderate switching, and interacting queues on random spatial carriers. Not claimed as solved here.
