THE QUESTION

A network of pools,
moving together.

Each node is a pool of parallel queues. A failure interrupts service at one pool, while the remaining network continues to move customers.

Model and assumptions in the paper ↗
01

Choose a destination

After service, a customer either leaves the network or is routed to an available node according to the current environmental phase.

02

Choose a queue

At the destination, sample d queues uniformly with replacement and join a shortest sampled queue.

03

Respond to failure

A failed pool keeps its customers and stops service until repair. Routing changes immediately with the environment.

A NOTE ON TERMINOLOGY

What does “pool” mean here?

A pool is a group of parallel, individually served queues at one network node. The shortest-of-d rule selects a queue inside that group. In the standard polling system, a server visits different queues according to a polling discipline. The manuscript studies the former model; links between these subjects can be developed separately.

Construction and assumptions

The demonstration has exactly one unavailable pool and removes its incident routes. This is a particular instance of the general model. In the ring example in §5 of the paper, pools in a residue class modulo four fail together and traffic is redirected to the next working pool; the simulator does not implement that example. The phase changes at rate γ/N in physical time, choosing the next unavailable pool uniformly. The case γ = 0 freezes the environment and lies outside the irreducibility assumption of Theorem 1. Edges touching this pool are set to zero. For active nodes, aᵢ = 1 − Σⱼpⱼᵢ and the exit probability is pᵢ₀ = 1 − Σⱼpᵢⱼ. External arrival rate is (N−1)aᵢ.

For a custom matrix, enter routing probabilities, not adjacency indicators. Rows and columns must each sum to strictly less than one. For generated graphs, χ controls the weights; for a CSV matrix its entries are used directly. Samples start from a clipped profile-based configuration; this is an approximate finite-N preparation, not an exact stationary sample.

How are P, h and the martingale term defined?

Routing. The entry pᵉᵢⱼ is the probability that a completion at active pool i is routed to active pool j. The laboratory starts with a base routing matrix and sets all entries incident to the failed pool to zero. The remaining probability is the probability of leaving the network.

aie=1−∑jpjie,pi0e=1−∑jpije.

For an active pool, the external arrival rate in physical time is (N−1)aᵉᵢ. The diagonal entries of Pᵉ are zero. For a ring the base probabilities are χ/2 to each neighbour; for a directed tandem they are χ along each forward edge; for a complete graph they are χ/(K−1). The sparse graph uses χ/max(deg i, deg j). An uploaded CSV supplies the base probabilities directly.

Profile function. The fractional part θ = {logdN} and the profile determine h:

h(x)=gθ(x)−1,gθ(x)=(d−1)dθω(x),ω=r−1.

The function r(w) is the mean of the integer-valued profile with tail probability exp(−wdʲ):

r(w)=∑j≥1e−wdj−∑j≤0(1−e−wdj).

The code numerically inverts r and interpolates gθ. Thus the displayed h values are numerical approximations.

Martingale noise. During a phase in which i is active, one representation of dMᵢ uses mutually independent Brownian motions Bᵢ and Bᵢⱼ for external events and directed routes:

dMi=ai+pi0dBi+∑jpjidBji−∑jpijdBij.

The Brownian route noise has opposite signs at its source and destination. For an active pool the total bracket rate is aᵢ+pᵢ₀+∑ⱼpⱼᵢ+∑ⱼpᵢⱼ = 2. Between two active pools, the cross-bracket rate is −(pᵢⱼ+pⱼᵢ). When a pool is down, both its drift and bracket rates are zero. The quantity dMᵢ is random, while these are its predictable covariance rates.

Failure and repair rates. In this laboratory exactly one of K pools is down. On accelerated time the whole phase changes at rate γ. An active specified pool fails at rate αᵢ=γ/(K−1), and the down pool is repaired at rate βᵢ=γ. Divide these rates by N for physical time. See the model and Theorem 1 in the paper.

The SDE is simulated numerically with the Euler–Maruyama scheme; gθ is interpolated. The displayed drift and weights are evaluated at the current numerical state.

THE MATHEMATICS

From discrete queues
to a switching diffusion.

Two results connect the finite queueing system with the behaviour of large networks. The paper states the precise assumptions and proofs.

THEOREM 1 · FIXED NETWORK

A diffusion for the
pool means.

Under critical loading, centered mean queue lengths and the environment converge jointly to a switching diffusion along subsequences of the fractional part of logdN. The empirical distribution in each pool tracks a profile indexed by its mean. A Brownian representation uses independent vertex and directed-route drivers whose combined covariance equals Γ; the theorem itself uses the symmetric positive semidefinite square root of Γ.

THE PAPER

Abstract

El'mira Yu. Kalimulina

Critical diffusion limits and a spatial convergence rate in queueing networks with unreliable nodes and two-stage dynamic routing

We study an open queueing network in which each node is a pool of N parallel queues. After service, a customer who remains in the network first chooses an available destination according to the current routing probabilities, then joins the shortest of d queues sampled there. A failed node holds its customers until repair. The offered load is 1-1/N per active node; the environmental transition rates scale as 1/N. We start from the joint stationary law of all queue lengths and the environmental phase specifying node availability and routing. Along subsequences on which the fractional part of log_d N converges, we prove joint convergence at times Nt of the centered node means and the phase to a switching diffusion and its driving chain. At repair, the first Wasserstein distance between the centered empirical queue-length distribution and the profile indexed by the centered mean at failure vanishes in probability. On growing, locally stabilizing graphs we obtain a stationary infinite-network limit on every fixed set of nodes. For the limiting diffusions, the Wasserstein error in the law of a node's trajectory decays exponentially in the radius of graph agreement. Under either a volume-growth bound or a bound on incoming routing weights, the numbers of nodes and queues per node may grow at arbitrary relative rates.
